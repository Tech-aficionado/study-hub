🔗 Joins Intermediate

How you combine rows from two related tables into one result.

1 INNER JOIN — only matching rows

SELECT users.name, orders.total
FROM users
INNER JOIN orders ON orders.user_id = users.id;
-- Only users who have at least one order appear in the result

2 LEFT JOIN — everything on the left, matched or not

SELECT users.name, orders.total
FROM users
LEFT JOIN orders ON orders.user_id = users.id;
-- EVERY user appears, even ones with zero orders — orders.total is NULL for them
💡 "Find all users who have NEVER placed an order" is a classic LEFT JOIN use case: LEFT JOIN orders ... WHERE orders.id IS NULL — the rows that didn't match on the right side.

3 RIGHT JOIN and FULL JOIN

RIGHT JOIN is the mirror of LEFT JOIN — everything from the right table, matched or not. In practice, most people just swap table order and use LEFT JOIN instead, since it's more intuitive to read.

FULL JOIN (or FULL OUTER JOIN) returns everything from BOTH tables, matched where possible, NULL-filled where not. Less common day-to-day, but essential for finding mismatches between two datasets.
